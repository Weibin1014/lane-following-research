OpenCV Vision: Meeting Notes
Based on the archived controller used for the completed driving trials.

1. Overall method
My current OpenCV controller uses colour segmentation and geometric checks to find the lane centre.
It detects the white and yellow markings, follows them across several image rows, and uses their positions to control steering.

2. Camera and image region
The images are 160 by 120 pixels, and the camera is configured for 20 frames per second.
The detector samples rows between about 55% and 90% of the image height, with roughly three pixels between rows.

3. Colour thresholds
I convert the image to HSV and apply separate thresholds for white and yellow.
White: H = 0–179, S = 0–80, V = 145–255.
Yellow: H = 15–40, S = 60–255, V = 80–255.
These thresholds were selected for the tested track and lighting conditions.

4. Noise removal
For the white mask, I use a horizontal 1-by-3 opening operation.
For the yellow mask, I use 3-by-3 opening and closing operations.
The current controller does not use the earlier Canny and Hough pipeline.

5. Boundary checks
I check that the white boundary is continuous across image rows.
I also check the distance between the yellow and white markings and whether the area between them looks like the dark road surface.
The allowed boundary spacing is 15% to 85% of the image width, or 24 to 136 pixels.
This is an image-space constraint, not a measured physical lane width.

6. Lane centre
When both boundaries are valid, I take the midpoint between them:
lane_centre_x = (white_x + yellow_x) / 2.
If direct pairing is unavailable, the controller tries a constrained boundary fit or a local centre-line fit.
The local fit requires at least three centre points and a maximum residual of 1.5 pixels, along with additional uncertainty checks.

7. Steering
The near target is at 65% of the image height.
The far target is at 55%, or 60% if the first target is unavailable.
The controller uses both the near-centre offset and the change between the near and far centres, so it can respond to bends.
The direction term has a weight of 1.5.
The steering PWM centre is 365, with a range of 290 to 440.
The forward throttle request is 400, and the stopped request is 370.

8. Centre-offset evaluation
The image centre is x = 80 pixels.
The code already computes a normalized near-centre offset:
near_offset = (near_centre_x - 80) / 80.
The signed pixel offset can therefore be recovered as:
pixel_offset = near_offset * 80.
The absolute distance is abs(pixel_offset).
However, I have not yet validated and summarized these values as a formal evaluation result.
The estimated lane centre also needs checking; a logged control value is not automatically an independent reference measurement.

9. Questions for the supervisor
Should I measure the horizontal pixel distance at a fixed image row, such as 65% of the image height?
Would manually marking the lane boundaries in sampled images be a suitable reference?
Should I report the mean absolute offset and the variation for each run?
For a fair comparison, should I record images from both controllers and apply the same evaluation method?
